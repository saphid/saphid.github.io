Keep the cache warm.

Coding agents build huge contexts. Then you go to lunch, the prompt cache expires, and your next message pays to write the whole thing again.

Alex Southwell4 minute readTwo weeks of real transcripts

The fix is a keep-alive. Just before the cache expires, resend the thread's last request byte for byte, with max_tokens: 0. You get no answer back. The provider reads the cached prefix, and that read resets the expiry clock. Drag the sliders above to see when it pays.

When it pays

Every ping is a cache read. Keep pinging long enough and the pings cost more than the rewrite. Context size cancels out, so break-even depends only on the price ratio and how often you ping. Fable 5.1 reads are 1/80th the price of a 1-hour write, which buys a lot of pings.

The catch: threads you never come back to

Break-even assumes you return. Often you don't. A keep-alive can't tell a lunch break from a finished task, so it pings abandoned threads too. That's what the cutoff is for. I replayed every idle gap in two weeks of my Claude Code transcripts, 9 to 23 September, including the 297 threads that went quiet for good.

The best cutoff was 3 hours: $215 saved, 7% of my $3,101 cache spend. Past 13 hours it loses money. At 24 hours it costs $185. Perfect foresight, pinging only threads I'd come back to, would have saved $399.

Every cold return

Each dot is one time I came back to an expired cache. Size is context. The cutoff slider above moves the line.

Don't shorten the cache

Pinging a 5-minute cache means a ping every 4 minutes. Most of my threads already used the 1-hour cache, so forcing it everywhere changed almost nothing. Forcing 5 minutes was a disaster.

What pings can't fix

A ping keeps the old prefix alive. It can't help once the prefix changes, whether from compaction, an edited system prompt, a model switch, or new tool definitions. In my data that barely happened.

Building it

  • Save the exact bytes of each idle thread's last request.
  • Replay them just before expiry, asking for zero output.
  • Stop after a few hours. The cutoff matters more than anything else.
  • Skip small contexts and subagents.
  • Keep pings out of thread history. They're cache traffic, not turns.

For a big thread you come back to after lunch, a few cents of pings saves a few dollars of rewrite. For a thread you've finished with, every ping is wasted money. Stop early.

Method: Claude Code transcripts, 9 to 23 September 2026, deduplicated by request. List prices from Anthropic's rate card: Fable 5.1 at $10/M input, $0.25/M cache read, $20/M 1-hour write. Pings every 55 minutes on 1-hour caches and 4 minutes on 5-minute ones. Contexts under 20k tokens are never pinged. Times are Sydney (AEST). Cache spend counts cache reads and writes only.
